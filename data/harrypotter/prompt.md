I am building a geography-based daily quiz game called **Pin5**.

I already have other Pin5 datasets, including an Airports game. I now want you to create a new dataset:

# Pin5: Harry Potter

This is a **public entertainment-geography quiz** about real-world places connected to the Harry Potter / Wizarding World franchise.

The player already knows they are playing a **Harry Potter quiz**.

The objective is NOT to identify a fictional location, character, or book/film title alone.

The objective is:

**Identify a real-world public place connected to Harry Potter — a filming location, studio tour site, theme-park land, landmark used in the films, premiere venue, exhibition site, or similar — from five progressively easier clues, then place a pin on the map as close as possible to that real place.**

Every map target must be a **real Earth location** that a general-audience quiz player can pin on a map.

Fictional places (Hogwarts, Diagon Alley, Platform 9¾, Hogsmeade, the Ministry of Magic, etc.) may appear in clues as Harry Potter knowledge, but they are **never** the map target. The pin always goes on the real-world place (Alnwick Castle for Hogwarts exteriors, Leadenhall Market for Diagon Alley, King's Cross Station for Platform 9¾, Universal's Wizarding World parks, Warner Bros. Studio Tour London, and so on).

There is **no fixed target number of locations**.

Build the largest high-quality Harry Potter Pin5 dataset that can reasonably be created from well-documented, meaningful geographical connections to **public / documented production and franchise places**.

Quantity must NEVER come at the expense of quality.

# 1. CORE GAME MECHANIC

Every location has exactly five clues.

The player starts with Clue 1 and can either guess the location on the map or reveal another clue.

Each clue should make the answer progressively easier.

The established Pin5 clue structure for this game is:

### Clue 1 — Hard Harry Potter fact

A difficult fact aimed at knowledgeable Harry Potter fans.

It should have a genuine connection to the real-world target location.

It should be interesting and specific rather than generic.

A serious fan might identify the place from this clue, but most players should need more information.

### Clue 2 — Easier Harry Potter fact / famous association

Give another Harry Potter-specific fact that makes the location easier to identify.

This should reveal meaningfully more information than Clue 1.

### Clue 3 — Strong narrowing clue

Give a clue that significantly narrows the answer.

This could identify or strongly indicate:

- book or film
- series era (early films, later films, *Fantastic Beasts*, stage / park)
- famous in-universe place portrayed there
- studio tour / theme park / attraction
- premiere or exhibition
- city or country
- well-known franchise landmark

A knowledgeable Harry Potter fan should now have a strong chance of identifying the real-world location.

### Clue 4 — Explicit answer

State the exact real-world location.

For example:

"The location is Alnwick Castle."

"The location is Leadenhall Market."

"The location is Warner Bros. Studio Tour London — The Making of Harry Potter."

Clue 4 MUST explicitly identify the real-world answer.

### Clue 5 — Geographical locating clue

Now help the player physically find the answer on the map.

For example:

"The location is in Alnwick, Northumberland, England."

"The location is in the City of London, England."

"The location is in Leavesden, Hertfordshire, England."

Clue 5 should make the geography easier but MUST NOT provide coordinates.

# 2. IMPORTANT: THIS IS ALREADY A HARRY POTTER GAME

Do NOT waste clues establishing that Harry Potter is involved.

Bad clues include:

"This location is associated with a famous wizarding franchise."

"A well-known fantasy film was shot here."

"This place has a connection to Harry Potter."

The player already knows that.

Every clue should provide useful information about **why this particular real-world place is connected to Harry Potter**.

Also:

Do NOT make the player pin a fictional place. If Clue 4 says "The location is Hogwarts," that is wrong. Clue 4 must name the real Earth place.

# 9. CLUE QUALITY

The clues are one of the most important parts of the dataset.

Avoid boring generic clues such as:

"Harry Potter was filmed here."

"This is a castle used in a movie."

"This location is in England."

"A famous wizarding franchise shot scenes here."

Instead use interesting Harry Potter-specific facts.

Clue 1 and Clue 2 should reward Harry Potter knowledge (films, scenes, in-universe places, production trivia).

Clue 3 should significantly narrow the possibilities.

Clue 4 gives the real-world answer.

Clue 5 helps locate that answer geographically.

The progression should feel like:

**Harry Potter deep cut → recognisable franchise fact → strong narrowing clue → real-world answer → map help**

Each clue must add new information.

Do not simply reword the same fact five times.

A strong pattern often looks like:

1. obscure production / scene detail
2. clearer in-universe place or famous scene
3. film / park / city narrowing
4. explicit real-world place name
5. country / region locating help
